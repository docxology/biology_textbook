Climate interpretation reference: warm/wet year-round conditions are compatible with tropical wet forest; a warm dry season with seasonal forest/savanna; very low rainfall with desert; temperate seasonal climate with seasonal forest; mild maritime high rainfall with temperate rainforest; long freezing winters with boreal vegetation; permafrost/short growing seasons with tundra; grass dominance plus drought with grassland/dry shrubland; winter rain/summer drought with winter-wet shrub/woodland. These are broad associations, not unique classifications. Thermal displacement=temporal warming rate/spatial temperature gradient, measured in the coordinate used for that gradient. Elevational displacement is not horizontal travel; terrain geometry and species ecology are needed before interpreting tracking requirements or risk. For flood adaptation, design a comparison of restored flood-storage habitat versus an embankment using matched flood exposure, maintenance, access/consent and biodiversity measures; no performance dataset here ranks one universally above the other.

## Paper-Based Investigation {.unnumbered}

1. Classify broad likely vegetation for all ten climate cards, using seasonality and stating ambiguity. Sketch temperature/precipitation axes. Annual means cannot uniquely assign every biome or resolve D versus I.
2. Fit logS=logc+zlogA to all six area-richness pairs. Predict 10000→100 ha loss and per-fragment richness at 129.6 ha. Do not sum ten predictions without species-overlap data.
3. Compute PVA persistence and the smallest tested census meeting95%. Evaluate every applicable IUCN criterion and choose the strongest category, not the first match. Distinguish genetic Ne from census size.
4. Compare reserve complements within budget10, respecting consent and refugia. Compute interior area/fraction. Contrast an ecosystem-based flood adaptation with hard engineering using risk, maintenance, rights and biodiversity endpoints; corridors do not solve every dispersal or disease problem.
5. Calculate horizontal thermal displacement, elevational displacement and toy DHW, labeling coordinates and dose units. State why mountain travel distance and relative extinction risk cannot be computed from these gradients alone. Screen duplicate, coordinate and uncertainty issues in all four occurrence records. Explain why presence-only records do not estimate abundance or absence and why uncertainty matters for fine-grid refugia.

## Data Recording {.unnumbered}

For each task record: given inputs with units; equation/assumption; intermediate arithmetic; result with units; replicate identifier; comparison/control; and one limitation. Draw graphs with labeled axes and retain individual observations rather than only a fitted line. Report range or sampleSD where replicates are supplied; do not invent confidence intervals from absent data.

## Analysis and Submission {.unnumbered}

Submit labeled diagrams and calculations for every numbered investigation task, a concise results table or graph, and a claim–evidence–mechanism paragraph. Close with the strongest alternative explanation, a discriminating control, and a transfer decision whose assumptions are explicit. Clearly distinguish synthetic observations from cited biological mechanisms.

## Safety and Ethics {.unnumbered}

This is a paper-only exercise. Do not collect human/animal specimens, administer drugs, conduct stimulation or release organisms. Synthetic clinical-style patterns are not diagnoses or treatment advice. Respect source attribution, participant privacy, local rights and ecological uncertainty.
