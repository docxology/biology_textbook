## Instructor Use and Coverage Notes {.unnumbered}

- **Coverage target:** Given a biological claim, identify the practice that produced it, the evidence standard it used, and one philosophical assumption it carries.
- **Model/data emphasis:** Concept-map and evidence-matrix comparisons among historical claims, mechanisms, and model assumptions.
- **Assessment alignment:** Questions and Methods, Representing and Describing Data, Argumentation.
- **Misconception probe:** History is not a list of dates; it explains why current biological concepts are powerful, limited, and revisable.
- **Transfer product:** Transfer source-analysis reasoning to modern debates about species, genetics, microbiomes, and biomedical ethics.
- **Grading focus:** award full credit for mechanism, evidence, boundary conditions, and units when a calculation is required.
- **Suggested use:** draw one recall item, one application item, and one synthesis item when building a short quiz from this bank.

## Questions 1–10 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
1. Define biology as an evidence practice rather than a list of facts.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q1, Recall).** Biology is an evidence practice because claims are produced through observation, experiment, classification, modeling, measurement, interpretation, and ethical judgement. A biological "fact" is stronger when we can name the method, unit, comparison, uncertainty, and possible revision path that produced it.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
2. Name one contribution of Aristotle to biological reasoning and one limitation of his approach.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q2, Recall).** Aristotle contributed systematic observation and functional explanation; a limitation is that teleological language can imply fixed purposes.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
3. What distinguishes natural history from experiment as a biological practice?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q3, Recall).** Natural history compares and classifies organisms across places and times, often revealing distribution, variation, and association. Experiment deliberately changes conditions and measures responses, making causal claims stronger when controls and comparison groups are well designed.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
4. State the core logic of natural selection in one sentence.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q4, Recall).** Heritable variation affects survival or reproduction in a particular environment, changing variant frequencies across generations.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
5. Define mechanistic explanation in biology.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q5, Recall).** A mechanistic explanation identifies entities, activities, organization, and causal sequence that produce a phenomenon.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
6. What is a biological species concept?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q6, Recall).** It defines species by reproductive isolation, with limits for asexual organisms, fossils, hybrids, and microbes.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO8; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
7. Why is nature-nurture a misleading frame for development?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q7, Recall).** It treats genes and environment as separable rivals instead of interacting developmental resources.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO9; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
8. Name two ways values can enter biological research without making evidence arbitrary.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q8, Recall).** Values can shape which questions are asked, how categories are defined, which risks are acceptable, and how uncertainty is communicated. Evidence is not arbitrary because claims still must answer to observations, controls, replication, and alternative explanations.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
9. Explain why an instrument such as a microscope can change biological knowledge.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q9, Recall).** An instrument changes what can be observed and measured, making new biological claims possible.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
10. Why should global biological traditions not be treated as a single pre-modern block?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q10, Recall).** Different traditions used different categories, practices, and aims; combining them erases that diversity.
SOLUTION -->

## Questions 11–20 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
11. Revise this statement: Harvey proved circulation by looking carefully at anatomy.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q11, Application).** Harvey combined anatomy with intervention and quantification: valves, tied vessels, pressure, and volume estimates supported circulation.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
12. A trait increases survival in one environment. What evidence is needed before calling it an adaptation?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q12, Application).** Evidence should show heritable variation, differential reproduction, selection pressure, and population change.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
13. Rephrase the heart wants to pump blood into a defensible functional statement.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q13, Application).** The heart does not literally want anything. A defensible causal-role version is: cardiac muscle contraction generates pressure gradients that move blood through pulmonary and systemic vessels. A selected-effect version would add that vertebrate hearts were shaped by selection because this circulation supported oxygen delivery, waste removal, and sustained activity.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
14. A ring species blurs boundaries between neighboring populations. Which species concept is challenged?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q14, Application).** It challenges a simple biological species concept because reproductive boundaries form a continuum.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO8; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
15. A variant is associated with height in one population. Why does that not prove height is fixed?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q15, Application).** The association depends on population, environment, measurement, development, and many interacting causes.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO9; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
16. A conservation team must decide whether two populations should be protected separately. Identify one empirical and one value-laden question.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q16, Application).** Empirical: gene flow or local adaptation. Value-laden: acceptable extinction risk, cost, or cultural importance.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
17. Classify a phylogenetic tree as observation, experiment, model, or classification, and justify your answer.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q17, Application).** It is a model-supported classification and historical inference built from observations and assumptions. The sequence or morphology data are observations; the tree is an inferred model of relationships under assumptions about character change, sampling, and method.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
18. Compare Aristotle functional reasoning with a modern mechanistic account of an organ.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q18, Application).** The functional account names role; the mechanistic account names tissues, activities, constraints, development, and history.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
19. Give one biological question better approached by natural history before experiment.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q19, Application).** Questions about range, seasonality, fossils, or ecological associations need pattern description first.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
20. Explain why Mendelian inheritance did not by itself produce the Modern Synthesis.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q20, Application).** The synthesis required population mathematics, selection, mutation, drift, systematics, and paleontology.
SOLUTION -->

## Integrated reasoning {.unnumbered}

## Questions 21–30 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
21. Evaluate the claim that organs having functions makes biology goal-directed.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q21, Synthesis).** Function language is useful when it means causal role or selected effect, but it misleads when it implies conscious goals, inevitable progress, or perfect design. Separate mechanism (how the organ works), development (how it forms), function (what contribution or selected effect it has), and evolutionary history (why that contribution became common).
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
22. Are humans biological individuals if their bodies depend on microbial communities?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q22, Synthesis).** Yes in many contexts, because legal, developmental, and physiological practices often treat a human body as one individual. But microbial dependence makes individuality partly context-dependent: for digestion, immunity, and disease ecology, the relevant unit may be a host-microbe system rather than a single genome.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO8; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
23. Critique the headline: scientists found the gene for intelligence.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q23, Synthesis).** An association at one locus does not establish a sole cause (1); cognitive measurements aggregate multiple traits and contexts (1); many variants and developmental environments contribute, so effects can vary across populations (1); a defensible headline names the measured score, cohort and association, then calls for replication or a mechanistic test (1). Do not infer individual destiny from population variance. See \cref{sec:unit_0_history_philosophy_biology}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO9; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
24. Should values be removed from conservation biology? Use a concrete decision.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q24, Synthesis).** No: protecting a wetland requires choosing acceptable extinction risk and weighing livelihood costs (1). Species abundance and hydrological measurements constrain likely effects (1), but do not themselves decide whose costs are acceptable (1). State stakeholder values and compare transparent alternatives under uncertainty (1). Evidence is not optional simply because the decision has values; see \cref{sec:unit_0_history_philosophy_biology}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
25. Design a four-column evidence map for a microbiome health claim.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q25, Synthesis).** A strong four-column map could use claim, biological unit, evidence practice, and revision test. For example: "a high-fiber diet improves microbiome health"; unit = host-microbe system rather than single species; evidence practice = association study, intervention trial, or mechanistic metabolomics; revision test = a controlled diet intervention that changes fiber without changing confounders and measures symptoms, taxa, and metabolites.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
26. Create a teaching example comparing a non-European biological tradition with a modern practice.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q26, Synthesis).** Compare Ibn Sina's organization of remedies and illnesses in the Canon of Medicine with a modern controlled drug trial \citep{ibnsinaCanon}. Identify its historical classificatory and therapeutic aims (1), contrast random allocation and specified outcome measures (1), separate contextual categories from modern molecular mechanisms without calling either timeless (1), and ask which observation would revise a treatment claim in each practice (1). Do not present the Canon as already a modern randomized trial; see \cref{sec:unit_0_history_philosophy_biology}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
27. Propose a source-card activity that distinguishes observation from interpretation.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q27, Synthesis).** Students underline observations, circle inferences, list missing comparisons, and write an alternative interpretation. A strong version also asks them to name the biological unit, the instrument or source practice, one value-laden decision, and one finding that would force revision.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
28. Evaluate whether survival of the fittest is a good beginner summary of natural selection.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q28, Synthesis).** The phrase can be circular if fittest means only whoever survived (1). Define fitness independently as expected reproductive contribution in a specified environment (1), require heritable variation and differential reproduction (1), and predict a frequency change rather than progress toward perfection (1). Survival without reproduction need not increase allele frequency; see \cref{sec:unit_0_history_philosophy_biology}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO6; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
29. Build a Tinbergen-style explanation for one behavior.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q29, Synthesis).** For bird migration: mechanism = photoperiod, circadian/circannual signaling, fat metabolism, navigation cues, and motor circuits; development = inherited program plus juvenile learning and calibration; function = seasonal movement can increase feeding or breeding success; evolutionary history = lineages with successful routes left more descendants under past climates. Keep the four answers distinct, then state which evidence would weaken each.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO9; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
30. Choose a later topic and identify its unit, model assumption, mechanism, and value-laden decision.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q30, Synthesis).** For antibiotic resistance: the unit might be a resistance gene, bacterium, patient microbiome, hospital ward, or regional population. The model assumption is selection under drug exposure; the mechanism may involve mutation, plasmids, efflux, target modification, or biofilm protection; the value-laden decision is stewardship that balances patient benefit against population-level resistance risk. Name the level of explanation and one observation that would force revision.
SOLUTION -->
