# Questions — Epigenetic Inheritance and Disease {#sec:q_unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease .unnumbered}


## Instructor Use and Coverage Notes {.unnumbered}

- **Coverage target:** Explain how 3D organization or inherited marks alter expression and disease risk.
- **Model/data emphasis:** Chromatin-loop, inheritance, and disease-risk reasoning.
- **Assessment alignment:** Concept Explanation, Questions and Methods, Argumentation.
- **Misconception probe:** A chromatin contact map is not proof of function without perturbation.
- **Transfer product:** Apply inheritance logic to cancer, developmental disorders, and environmental exposure.
- **Grading focus:** award full credit for mechanism, evidence, boundary conditions, and units when a calculation is required.
- **Suggested use:** draw one recall item, one application item, and one synthesis item when building a short quiz from this bank.

*This activity accompanies \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.*

## Concepts and evidence {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
1. Define a TAD and distinguish it from an A/B compartment.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q1, Recall).** A TAD is a region enriched for internal chromatin contacts relative to flanks; compartments reflect broader segregation of chromatin with similar state. Award one point for each distinction; neither is simply a permanent membrane-bounded organelle. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
2. What does a Hi-C read pair measure, and what functional conclusion does it not establish?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q2, Recall).** It records a ligation product between spatially proximate fragments in a population assay. It does not by itself establish a stable loop, enhancer function, or causal transcriptional regulation. Two points: measurement and boundary. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
3. Distinguish DNA-binding specificity from the proposed contribution of a transcriptional condensate.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q3, Recall).** Sequence-specific TF binding selects regulatory sites; multivalent interactions can locally concentrate cofactors beyond simple occupancy. Award both mechanisms; puncta alone do not prove liquid–liquid phase separation. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
4. Name the two major mammalian methylation-resetting windows and state when imprints are normally erased.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q4, Recall).** Post-fertilization and primordial-germ-cell reprogramming are distinct windows. Many imprints are protected during early embryonic resetting but normally erased in PGCs, then reset by germline sex. Three points; do not say imprints always escape PGC erasure. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
5. Distinguish mitotic memory, intergenerational exposure effects, and transgenerational inheritance.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q5, Recall).** Mitotic memory persists across cell divisions; intergenerational effects can involve directly exposed offspring/germ cells; transgenerational inheritance requires persistence into unexposed generations. Award each distinction, not merely the word heritable. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
6. How can a tumor have global hypomethylation and focal promoter hypermethylation simultaneously?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q6, Recall).** Different loci can change in opposite directions: repeat hypomethylation can weaken genome repression while selected promoter CpG islands gain methylation and lose expression. Award locus specificity and two consequences; an average methylation value cannot resolve them. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
7. Contrast DNMT inhibition with targeted dCas9-TET1 editing.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q7, Recall).** DNMT inhibition can dilute methylation during replication across many loci; TET1 fusion directs local cytosine oxidation/demethylation with guide-dependent targeting. Award scale and mechanism; targeted does not mean free of off-target activity or clinically approved. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
8. Define objective response rate, progression-free survival, and duration of CR+CRh.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q8, Recall).** ORR is the fraction meeting a specified tumor-response definition; PFS is time to protocol-defined progression or death; duration of CR+CRh is time that the specified complete-remission response persists among responders. Three points; these are not interchangeable denominators. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

## Data and mechanism applications {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
9. Under P(s) proportional to s^-1, what is the contact frequency at 500 kb relative to 50 kb? Can the result be called an absolute probability?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q9, Application).** The ratio is(500/50)^-1=0.1. It is a relative frequency under the stated model, not an absolute10% contact probability. Three points: ratio, normalization, and model qualification. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
10. Synthetic matched contact counts per 10000 pairs are 10,12,8 in controls and 40,44,36 after boundary deletion; repaired cells are 12,10,8. Compute means/fold change and interpret repair.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q10, Application).** Means10,40,10 give a fourfold deletion increase and return to baseline after repair. Isogenic rescue strengthens causal boundary inference, but expression/viability and off-target controls are needed before claiming enhancer hijacking. Four points: arithmetic, rescue, functional limitation, control. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
11. A compound reduces puncta from 100 to 20 per 100 cells and RNA to 40% of baseline, while viability falls to 60%. What confound prevents a condensate-specific interpretation?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q11, Application).** Cell loss/stress can reduce puncta and RNA independently of condensate disruption. Normalize viable cells, use matched toxicity controls and an orthogonal separation-of-function perturbation; measure DNA binding and transcription. Four points; do not treat puncta disappearance as proof of LLPS. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
12. An F0 dam is exposed during pregnancy. Identify directly exposed generations and the first generation suitable for an unexposed-generation test.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q12, Application).** F0 is exposed; F1 fetus and its future F2 germ cells are directly exposed. F3 is the first unexposed generation in this design. Three points, with exposure timing stated; F2 persistence alone is not sufficient. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
13. An F0 male is exposed before conception, then breeds with an unexposed female. Which descendant generation is the first unexposed test?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q13, Application).** F1 derives from exposed paternal germ cells; F2 is the first unexposed generation under the stated no-further-exposure design. Award generation and explicit assumption; this differs from gestational maternal exposure. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO6; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
14. F3 methylation means are .20 in exposed-lineage descendants and .10 in controls, with phenotype means 12 versus 10. All share standard diet. What can and cannot be inferred?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q14, Application).** The association persists into an unexposed generation, but sequence variation, maternal care, selection and cell composition can still explain it. Award observed association, at least two alternatives, and an independent pedigree or rescue control; persistence alone does not prove the mark is causal. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
15. At the IGF2/H19 locus, an unmethylated maternal ICR binds CTCF. Predict the effect of maternal ICR hypermethylation without assuming a particular deletion.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q15, Application).** Reduced CTCF binding can weaken insulation and permit maternal IGF2 enhancer access, increasing IGF2 while altering H19 regulation. Three points: mark, insulator, parent-specific expression. A deletion is not equivalent to every methylation lesion. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
16. A tumor loses MLH1 expression and has promoter hypermethylation and microsatellite instability. Give a plausible causal chain and a discriminating experiment.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q16, Application).** Methylation could repress MLH1, reducing mismatch repair and increasing repeat-length errors. Targeted demethylation with catalytic-dead and non-target guides, followed by MLH1 protein and functional repair assays, tests the chain. Four points; association does not exclude an MLH1 coding defect. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
17. A synthetic cytosine-fraction model has f0=.9 and maintenancep=0 with no de novo methylation. Find f after3 divisions and state why this does not predict response time in a patient.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q17, Application).** f=.9/8=.1125. Patient exposure, division rates, clonal selection and other repression layers differ from the model. Three points: arithmetic, observable, and at least one clinical limit. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
18. After drug washout, f0=.10 and maintenancep=.95, with no de novo methylation. Can maintenance alone restore f to.90?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q18, Application).** No. The recurrence multiplies by .975 each division, so f declines rather than recovering; after 10 divisions it is .10(.975)^10≈.0776. Restoring lost sites requires de novo or other directed activity. Four points; maintenance is not de novo methylation. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
19. The historical single-arm sarcoma cohort had 9 responders among 62 patients and median PFS 5.5 months. Calculate ORR and explain why 5.5 versus 1.9 months cannot be called a placebo benefit.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q19, Application).** 9/62=14.5%, reported rounded15%. There was no placebo arm;1.9months was a response-time IQR bound, not controlPFS. Three points: denominator, design, endpoint separation. The original study supports historical activity, not a randomized treatment effect \citep{gounder2020tazemetostat}. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
20. INDIGO reports next-interventionHR=.26. Calculate the hazard reduction and explain why it is not the percent reduction in waiting time.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q20, Application).** 1-.26=.74=74% lower hazard. A hazard compares instantaneous event rates under a model; it is not a ratio of elapsed times or medians. Three points, including direction: benefit means delaying intervention, not shortening waiting time \citep{fda2024vorasidenib}. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
21. AUGMENT-101 FDA evidence gives 22/104 CR+CRh and median CR+CRh duration 6.4 months. Why is attaching 6.4 months to an ORR 53% from another report invalid?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q21, Application).** 22/104=21.15%≈21.2%, specifically CR+CRh in the 104-patient cohort. ORR includes other response classes and may use a different cohort/cutoff. Award calculation, named endpoint, and denominator/cutoff requirement; the 6.4-month result belongs to CR+CRh \citep{fda2024revumenib}. See \cref{sec:unit_IV_epigenetic_inheritance_and_disease}.
