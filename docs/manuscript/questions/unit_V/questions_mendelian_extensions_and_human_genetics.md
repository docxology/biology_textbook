# Questions — Mendelian Extensions and Human Genetics {#sec:q_unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics .unnumbered}


## Instructor Use and Coverage Notes {.unnumbered}

- **Coverage target:** Use pedigree or extension evidence to justify an inheritance diagnosis.
- **Model/data emphasis:** Pedigree, epistasis, and multi-locus probability calculations.
- **Assessment alignment:** Statistical Tests and Data Analysis, Representing and Describing Data.
- **Misconception probe:** A single-gene model is a starting hypothesis, not the default for every trait.
- **Transfer product:** Transfer extension reasoning to counseling, GWAS interpretation, and breeding.
- **Grading focus:** award full credit for mechanism, evidence, boundary conditions, and units when a calculation is required.
- **Suggested use:** draw one recall item, one application item, and one synthesis item when building a short quiz from this bank.

*This bank accompanies \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}. Synthetic counts below are teaching examples, not patient records.*

## Questions 1–10 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
1. Distinguish incomplete dominance from codominance.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q1, Recall).** Incomplete dominance produces an intermediate heterozygote phenotype; codominance makes both allelic products detectable. Pink versus simultaneous A/B antigens are different observations, not different segregation rules. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
2. Why can four alleles produce ten diploid genotypes but only four phenotypes under a strict dominance hierarchy?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q2, Recall).** There are 4 homozygotes and 6 unordered heterozygotes. Each genotype expresses the highest-ranking allele it contains, giving four phenotype classes. Ten genotypes do not imply ten phenotypes. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
3. Define epistasis and distinguish it from dominance.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q3, Recall).** Dominance concerns alleles at one locus; epistasis concerns how genotype at one locus modifies expression at another. Labrador ee masks the B/b pigment difference. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
4. Define penetrance and expressivity.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q4, Recall).** Penetrance is the fraction of a genotype class meeting a phenotype definition; expressivity is the degree of phenotype among expressers. State age and ascertainment context. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Remember; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
5. What is a phenocopy?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q5, Recall).** A phenotype resembles a genetic condition but results from another cause, often an exposure. Absence of a known variant alone does not prove an environmental cause because testing can miss variants. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO6; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
6. Distinguish locus heterogeneity from polygenic inheritance.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q6, Recall).** Locus heterogeneity means different loci can independently cause a similar phenotype in different families. Polygenic inheritance involves contributions from many loci to variation within a population; those claims require different evidence. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
7. What does maternally imprinted IGF2 mean in the simplified dosage model?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q7, Recall).** The maternal allele is silenced and the paternal allele expressed. Explicitly naming the silent allele prevents confusing imprinting with expression. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
8. What does a GWAS association establish?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q8, Recall).** It associates a marker with a trait in a specified dataset after an analysis model. Linkage disequilibrium, confounding and ascertainment prevent identifying the marker as causal from association alone. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
9. Distinguish PRS discrimination from calibration.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q9, Recall).** Discrimination ranks cases above noncases; calibration compares predicted probabilities with observed outcomes. A well-ranked score can systematically overestimate absolute risk. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Understand; difficulty=Recall; format=short-answer; minutes=2 -->
10. Why does an affected father and affected son not by itself exclude X-linked recessive inheritance?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q10, Recall).** The son receives his X from his mother. A carrier mother can produce affected sons even when the father is affected; phenotype co-occurrence is not paternal X transmission. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

## Questions 11–20 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
11. For BbEe × BbEe, B_E_ is black, bbE_ chocolate and ee yellow. Derive the ratio.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q11, Application).** Independent loci give 9/16 B_E_, 3/16 bbE_ and 4/16 ee: 9 black:3 chocolate:4 yellow. The 4 merges B_ee and bbee, not a change in gamete segregation. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
12. For complementary genes with A_B_ purple and all other classes white, calculate F2 proportions from AaBb selfing.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q12, Application).** Purple is (3/4)(3/4)=9/16; white is 7/16. A linear pathway needing both products is compatible, but the ratio alone does not uniquely prove pathway order. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
13. Tt × Tt produces a dominant tailless phenotype, but TT embryos die. Predict living offspring.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q13, Application).** Conceptions are 1/4 TT,1/2 Tt,1/4 tt. Conditional on survival, 2/3 are tailless Tt and 1/3 tailed tt. The 2:1 living ratio does not mean TT was never conceived. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
14. An unaffected woman has an aa father under complete penetrance of a recessive disease. Her partner is Aa. Compute carrier and child risk.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q14, Application).** She must be Aa because her father supplies a and unaffected excludes aa. Carrier probability is 1; the Aa × Aa child risk is 1/4 per conception, independent of previous children. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
15. An unaffected sibling of an affected child has two known Aa parents. Compute carrier probability and affected-child risk with an Aa partner.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q15, Application).** Conditional carrier probability is (1/2)/(3/4)=2/3. Risk is (2/3)(1/4)=1/6, assuming the partner carries the same disease locus and complete penetrance. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
16. A genotype is found in 80 people; 48 show a defined phenotype at age 40. Estimate penetrance and an approximate standard error.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q16, Application).** Penetrance=48/80=0.60; binomial SE=sqrt(0.6×0.4/80)≈0.0548. This cross-sectional estimate is not lifetime penetrance; selection of participants can bias it. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
17. A stipulated 3:1 cross gives 450 dominant and 130 recessive offspring. Calculate chi-square (critical 3.84, df=1).

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q17, Application).** Expected 435 and 145; chi-square=225/435+225/145=2.069. Do not reject at α=.05; compatibility is not proof that chance caused the discrepancy. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
18. A type O child has an I^A i mother. List compatible paternal ABO genotypes and the possible children for each.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q18, Application).** Father ii gives 1/2 A,1/2 O; I^A i gives 3/4 A,1/4 O; I^B i gives 1/4 A,B,AB,O each. These are conditional possibilities, not a unique paternal blood type or a parentage determination. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
19. Generation I father X^aY,Aa and mother X^AX^a,Aa have a daughter X^AX^a,Aa in generation II. She mates with X^AY,aa. For generation III calculate a child affected at both independent recessive loci.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q19, Application).** The daughter produces an affected son at the X locus with probability 1/4 per child; Aa×aa gives autosomal aa with probability 1/2. Independent loci give 1/8 per child, or 1/4 conditional on being a son. The paternal X from generation I could pass only to daughters. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Apply; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
20. For an illustrative log-odds score β1g1+β2g2, let β1=0.2, β2=−0.1 and the two dosages be 2 and 1, respectively. Compute the score and odds multiplier.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q20, Application).** Score=0.4−0.1=0.3; the odds multiplier is e^0.3≈1.35. It is not a 35-percentage-point absolute-risk increase; an intercept and calibrated baseline are missing. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

## Questions 21–30 {.unnumbered}

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
21. In a synthetic external validation, 20 of 100 people assigned 10% risk develop disease. Is the score calibrated? What should change before clinical use?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q21, Synthesis).** Observed risk is 20%, twice the predicted 10%. Recalibrate and validate on independent target-setting data; assess uncertainty, discrimination and action thresholds. A different ancestry label alone neither explains nor fixes the error. Credit the denominator, direction and validation plan. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO6; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
22. One family has a variant segregating with disease; unrelated cases carry many different loci. Decide between monogenic, heterogeneous and polygenic claims.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q22, Synthesis).** A family can support a monogenic hypothesis while unrelated families show locus heterogeneity. Demonstrating polygenicity needs joint multi-locus contributions rather than just many candidate genes across cases. Require segregation, functional and population evidence; do not generalize a single pedigree. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO3; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
23. Design a study distinguishing incomplete penetrance from shared environmental phenocopies.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q23, Synthesis).** Recruit genotype-positive and genotype-negative relatives with age-matched phenotype assessment; measure exposure without knowing genotype; use independent families and blinded scoring. Genotype-associated risk within exposure strata supports penetrance; exposure-associated cases lacking the genotype support phenocopies. Testing limits and shared ancestry remain alternatives. Credit comparison groups and an explicit discriminating prediction. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO5; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
24. Unaffected parents have two affected sons and three unaffected daughters; an affected son has an unaffected daughter. Does this uniquely identify X-linked recessive inheritance?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q24, Synthesis).** No. A carrier mother plus unaffected father fits XLR; Aa×Aa fits autosomal recessive inheritance. An affected XLR father passes his allele to every daughter, but an unaffected daughter is a carrier only under the stated full-penetrance model. Additional genotypes, informative meioses and unbiased family ascertainment discriminate; sex imbalance alone is weak evidence. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
25. Design complementation and epistasis tests for recessive loss-of-function mutants, and identify their limits.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q25, Synthesis).** Cross mutants: wild-type F1 supports different genes, mutant F1 supports allelism under recessive assumptions. Make double mutants and compare to each single mutant and wild type. Ordering requires a specified activating/inhibitory pathway and null alleles; epistasis does not universally identify the downstream gene. Credit controls, independent alleles and rescue. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
26. Use the 2019 Martin perspective to evaluate a claim that a fixed cross-ancestry PRS accuracy gap is universal in 2026.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q26, Synthesis).** The perspective documents ancestry-dependent performance of scores available in 2019; it cannot establish a universal contemporary magnitude. Require a named score, endpoint, metric, cohort, ancestry representation and independent current validation. Credit the dated evidence boundary rather than a fabricated newer benchmark \citep{martin2019prsdisparities}. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO4; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
27. Design a follow-up to a noncoding GWAS association for height.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q27, Synthesis).** Fine-map with ancestry-aware LD and conditional analysis, test expression/chromatin effects in relevant growth cells, perturb candidate regulatory alleles with isogenic controls and rescue, then replicate. Association at one SNP is not a gene count or proof of causation. Credit orthogonal functional evidence and alternative linked variants. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO2; bloom=Analyze; difficulty=Application; format=short-answer; minutes=5 -->
28. A true Rr×rr testcross has 3 round and 1 wrinkled offspring. Is that impossible? Calculate its probability.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q28, Application).** P(exactly 3 round)=choose(4,3)(1/2)^4=1/4. The expected population ratio is 1:1, not a requirement on every four-offspring sample. No extra parent or contamination is implied. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO7; bloom=Create; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
29. Draft a responsible PRS consent and reporting plan.

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q29, Synthesis).** State discovery and validation cohorts, absolute-risk calibration and uncertainty, clinical action criteria and alternatives. Obtain consent for storage, secondary use, family implications and reidentification risk; offer counseling and equitable access. Explain that a score is not a diagnosis and legal protections differ across insurance/employment domains. Credit both scientific limits and specific governance decisions. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->

<!-- assess: LO=LO1; bloom=Evaluate; difficulty=Synthesis; format=short-answer; minutes=8 -->
30. Under paternal-only IGF2 expression, compare deletion of the paternal coding allele with deletion of the maternal coding allele; may these automatically be named complex imprinting syndromes?

<!-- SOLUTION
**Answer (Q30, Synthesis).** Losing the active paternal copy lowers IGF2 dosage and predicts reduced growth in this single-gene model. Losing the already silent maternal copy does not increase dosage. Broad 11p15 control-region lesions can affect other genes and must be specified before syndrome attribution. Credit active-copy accounting and the boundary between model and diagnosis. See \cref{sec:unit_V_mendelian_extensions_and_human_genetics}.
SOLUTION -->
